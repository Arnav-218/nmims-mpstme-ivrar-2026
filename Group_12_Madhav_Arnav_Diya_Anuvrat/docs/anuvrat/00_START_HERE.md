## 1. What Our Project Is About (In Plain English)

When employees or students get an email that says *"URGENT: Your account will be suspended in 2 hours unless you click here to verify your password,"* many people panic and click the link without checking who actually sent it or where the link actually goes. This type of scam is called **phishing**.

Organizations try to stop this by making employees watch 2D training slides or web videos once a year. While people usually pass the quiz right after watching the video (**Day 0**), they quickly forget the lesson after two weeks (**Day 14**).

Our project tests a research question:
> **"To what extent does an immersive VR phishing simulation improve threat recognition and mitigate cognitive bias compared to standard 2D web-based cybersecurity training across a 14-day retention interval?"**

Instead of watching flat 2D slides, participants in the **Immersive VR** group sit inside a 3D virtual office in Unity VR, inspect incoming emails on a virtual monitor, pull a physical alarm lever to report phishing emails, and have their **eye movements (eye gaze)** tracked to measure whether they actually look at the technical security indicators (the sender's domain address, security padlock badge, and hyperlink destination URL).

## 3. Critical Note on Data Provenance (Synthetic vs. Real Data)

The repository contains two CSV files in `telemetry/`:
1. `telemetry/phishing_simulation_benchmark.csv` (`N = 50` rows: 25 in `2D_Web_Training`, 25 in `Immersive_VR_Training`).
2. `telemetry/sample_experiment_data.csv` (`N = 18` rows: generic course onboarding dataset).

**IMPORTANT:** Both CSV files currently in the repository are **synthetic benchmark/sample datasets** generated by computer code (`random.gauss` with `seed = 42`), **not** measurements collected from 50 real human participants. Every script, figure, and report in your pipeline explicitly labels this provenance so that synthetic benchmark numbers are never misrepresented as real human experimental evidence.
